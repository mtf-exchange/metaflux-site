01

Abstract

MetaFlux is an independent Layer 1 built for one job: running a derivatives exchange. The central limit order book and clearinghouse execute inside consensus as a single deterministic state machine; an inline EVM (revm-based) shares the same block for programmable strategies; MetaBridge, a validator-cosigned bridge, moves assets in and out; and one gateway front door serves the MTF-native REST/WS surface plus EVM JSON-RPC. MetaFlux runs its own validator set, its own token, and its own bridge, and joins no external mainnet.

This is not a paper design. Perpetuals and spot trade on a public devnet today, with tiered liquidation, SPAN-style portfolio margin, staking, vaults, and derivatives-aware precompiles live behind the gateway, and the bridge attested end-to-end on its first corridors. The wager is that lower fees are a commodity and capability is not. MetaFlux differentiates on three axes a serious trader actually feels: market microstructure (MEV-resistant matching modes, RFQ, frequent batch auctions, on-chain market-making primitives), risk and margin (cross-asset portfolio margin, a graduated liquidation ladder, regret-minimizing auto-deleveraging), and the execution layer (a Block-STM parallel EVM with derivatives-aware precompiles).

This paper is precise about state. Every feature carries the same status tag its documentation carries — stable (wire committed for V1), live on devnet, preview (works today; minor wire changes possible before mainnet), or planned (described, not shipped). Where a number appears, it is a devnet or benchmark figure, never a production claim.

02

Thesis: capability over price

The perpetuals market has converged on a familiar competition: shave a fraction of a basis point off taker fees, subsidize volume with token emissions, clone a vault to bootstrap liquidity. That race has a floor at zero and a predictable winner — whoever can burn the most capital. MetaFlux declines to run it.

A professional desk does not choose a venue to save 0.2 bps a fill. It chooses a venue where a hedged book margins to one number instead of three, where a large order does not advertise itself before it fills, where a smart contract can read live order-book depth without paying 80,000 gas to reimplement it. Those are structural properties of the protocol, not line items in a fee schedule, and they compound.

So the differentiation is capability-driven and concentrated on three axes:

Axis 1 / Microstructure

Advanced order types and per-market matching modes that resist ordering-based extraction: RFQ for size, frequent batch auctions where fairness should beat latency, threshold-encrypted order intents so inclusion is fixed before contents are readable.

Axis 2 / Risk & margin

A SPAN-style portfolio-margin engine live on devnet, dynamic risk parameters, a tiered liquidation ladder with a one-block grace tier, and auto-deleveraging reframed as an online-learning problem with a provable regret bound.

Axis 3 / Execution

A Block-STM parallel EVM so contract calls do not queue behind each other, and derivatives-aware precompiles that expose clearing and risk primitives to Solidity at native cost.

What is explicitly not on the list: the lowest fee, the most aggressive token subsidy, a liquidity-bombing house vault. Consensus throughput is also not a competition axis — HyperBFT is production-hardened and years live, and MetaFlux does not pretend to out-engineer it on raw consensus speed. MetaFlux competes one level up, on the microstructure and risk layer that sits above a safety kernel of equivalent guarantees.

03

Architecture

MetaFlux is a single L1 in which the exchange and a general-purpose EVM share one block. Each consensus round produces one unified block: exchange actions and EVM transactions execute together — there is no separate slow lane — and Core ↔ EVM transfers settle as deterministic, exactly-once system transactions. Around the L1 sit MetaBridge for inbound and outbound assets and a single gateway front door for clients.

3.1 MetaFluxBFT consensus live

Consensus is MetaFluxBFT — a MetaFlux-native BFT proof-of-stake engine in the HotStuff / Jolteon family, the line that includes DiemBFT: leader-based, partially synchronous, pipelined, with a two-chain commit rule. It is built from scratch on those public research foundations rather than forked, to satisfy the determinism and code discipline the rest of the chain demands.

The design rule is non-negotiable: the safety kernel never changes. Every MetaFlux-specific feature lives strictly above it — in admission, in proposal validity, or in begin-block effects — so the chain inherits HotStuff's formally understood safety while still differentiating.

ElementMetaFluxBFT
Commit ruleTwo-chain, pipelined. Finality is immediate and absolute: zero confirmations, no probabilistic settlement, no reorg window.
QuorumAt least two-thirds of total staked voting power; voting is stake-weighted.
Validator setFixed within an epoch, changes at epoch boundaries; the leader role rotates across the set.
AccountabilityProvable misbehaviour is jailed and slashed; sustained unavailability is jailed (§5.5).
Signaturessecp256k1 over an EIP-712 envelope in the MetaFlux domain.
State commitmentA per-block app_hash — a compact fingerprint of resulting state, compared across validators every block.
ExecutionDeterministic by construction: floating point, unordered iteration, and wall-clock time are prohibited in the consensus path.

Immediate finality matters more for a derivatives venue than for a general-purpose chain: liquidations, funding, and bridge releases all settle against finalized state, so nothing downstream ever has to model a reorg.

3.2 Inline EVM live (devnet)

The EVM domain is revm-based and lives inside the same block — one unified block per consensus round, sub-second, with contract deploys confirming in the next block. There are no separate "small" and "large" block lanes: a single EIP-1559 fee market whose base fee is burned, an aggregate block limit, and a per-transaction gas cap absorb that role. Addresses are standard 20-byte EVM addresses derived from secp256k1 keys; signing is EIP-712; standard eth_* JSON-RPC is served at /evm. Every EVM wallet, hardware signer, and quant framework works unmodified.

3.3 MetaBridge live (testnet corridors) · pre-audit

Assets cross in and out through MetaBridge — all of them, including USDC. There is deliberately no third-party bridge and no Circle CCTP dependency on the critical path: the bridge's trust assumption is exactly the chain's own. Inbound deposits are attested by validators and credited on a two-thirds stake-weighted quorum, idempotent by a deterministic message id. Outbound withdrawals are co-signed to the same two-thirds threshold — with the same secp256k1 keys that secure consensus — then pass a dual dispute window (300 seconds and 150 blocks) before anyone can claim on the destination chain.

There is no admin account anywhere in the design. Any single hot validator can pause deposits or dispute a suspect withdrawal; only a two-thirds cold-key quorum can unpause, invalidate a withdrawal, or rotate the validator set, and hot/cold key separation with two-phase rotation keeps compromise recoverable. Corridors: Base (live on Base Sepolia), Solana (live on devnet), Arbitrum planned. The bridge is pre-audit and explicitly marked not for value-bearing use until the external audit lands — it is treated, correctly, as the highest-priority surface to close before mainnet.

3.4 Gateway live (devnet)

One gateway front door per network serves the entire client surface: POST /info for reads, POST /exchange for EIP-712-signed actions, GET /ws for streaming, POST /evm for EVM JSON-RPC, and a faucet route that is structurally never mounted on mainnet. TLS terminates at the gateway; the node is deliberately not internet-facing, and gateway-to-node traffic runs over mTLS. A self-hosted node serves the identical native surface directly.

MetaFlux speaks its own MTF-native protocol — there is deliberately no Hyperliquid-compatible shim. For a desk migrating a bot, the concepts port unchanged (agent wallets, sub-accounts, vaults, margin modes, client order ids); the wire layer is a client rewrite the SDKs absorb — TypeScript and Rust today, both preview — and the wire is small enough to hand-implement from the typed-data signing spec.

04

Axis 1 — Market microstructure

The order book is a price-time-priority CLOB matched deterministically inside the block. On top of that base, MetaFlux adds order types and matching modes aimed at the two things microstructure actually decides: who gets to extract value from ordering, and how large or sensitive flow finds a counterparty.

4.1 MEV-resistant matching modes preview

A deterministically ordered block removes MEV within a block, but ordering itself can still carry information. MetaFlux's answer is a per-market menu rather than one global mechanism, all clearing through the single core matching path: threshold-encrypted order intents, so an order's contents stay sealed until its inclusion is already fixed (the submit action is wired into the EVM write path today); frequent batch auctions (§4.2), where a uniform price makes intra-batch ordering worthless; and verifiable fair ordering for continuous markets, seeded from the parent block so a proposer cannot bias placement. Markets default to the continuous book and opt in per market.

4.2 Frequent batch auctions preview

For markets where fairness should beat latency, orders collected within a batch interval — default one second, ten consensus blocks — clear at a single uniform price chosen to maximize crossed volume, with the over-supplied side filled pro-rata and price ties resolved toward the prior mark. FBA neutralizes the latency arms race that lets the fastest co-located bot tax everyone else, and it composes cleanly with encrypted intents — a batch that is both sealed and uniform-price is hostile ground for an ordering-based extractor. A live read exposes the batch pool and an indicative clearing price while the window is open. Honest status: the read surface is live; the FBA order action itself is still a recognized-but-unmapped stub on the wire.

4.3 RFQ preview

Size that would move the book if it rested on it should not have to. A taker publishes a request — side, size, reference price, slippage bound, a time-to-live of a few seconds — and registered makers answer with firm quotes only the taker can see. Accepting settles atomically at the quoted price in the next block; the fill prints to the public tape after the fact, tagged rfq. A large hedge clears without telegraphing itself one level at a time, and RFQ book depth is exposed to smart contracts through a precompile (§6.2). Same honest status as FBA: the reads are live, the quote and accept actions are wire stubs today.

4.4 Order types & on-chain market making stable / live (devnet)

The base toolkit is stable on the wire: GTC, IOC, and FOK, plus ALO post-only orders that reject rather than cross and never pay taker fees; trigger orders that survive restarts; OCO grouping for entry-plus-bracket and position-attached TP/SL; TWAP execution with randomized slice jitter; scale orders; and a full set of self-trade-prevention modes. For market makers specifically, a dead-man's-switch scheduled cancel keeps quotes safe across disconnects, and market-data precompiles expose best bid/offer, aggregated L2 depth, and an inventory-risk read to Solidity at native cost — an on-chain market-making contract reads canonical book state instead of reconstructing it.

05

Axis 2 — Risk & margin

Risk infrastructure is where a derivatives venue earns or loses the trust of professional capital. MetaFlux treats margin, liquidation, and deleveraging as the differentiated surface they are.

5.1 Cross-asset portfolio margin live (devnet)

A SPAN-style scenario engine evaluates the whole book across a 24-scenario grid — price shocks of ±5, 10, and 20 percent crossed with volatility shocks of ±20 and 50 percent, plus a convexity term — and charges margin on the worst case, with a concentration penalty when any single asset exceeds half the portfolio's net value. A trader who is long one correlated asset and short another sees the offset in one margin number rather than paying full isolated margin on each leg; typical capital efficiency for hedged, multi-leg books is 2–5×. Enrollment is opt-in and reversible above a 100,000 USDC equity floor, and the same engine is exposed to smart contracts via the portfolio-margin precompile. Live on devnet and exercised on a multi-node consensus run; multi-collateral portfolio margin exists behind a governance gate and is not yet enabled.

5.2 Tiered liquidation stable

Most on-chain liquidation engines fire the moment an account crosses the maintenance boundary. That single tick of mark price then depresses the book, which trips neighbouring accounts across the same boundary, which depresses the book further — the cascade depth is mostly a function of how many accounts cluster at the edge when the shock lands. MetaFlux inserts a grace tier above the cliff, and bounds every step below it.

TierHealth bandAction
T0 — yellow card1.0 ≤ health < 1.1Force-cancel post-only (ALO) resting orders to free parked collateral; warn the wallet. No position is touched.
T1 — partial0.8 ≤ health < 1.0Partial close via price-floored IOC limit — never a market sweep; 30-second cooldown between partials.
T2 — full0.667 ≤ health < 0.8Full close, same price floor.
T3 — nettinghealth < 0.667Position transferred at the committed mark to the most profitable opposite-side accounts — no book interaction, no fees.
T4 — deficit waterfallnegative equityADL haircut (§5.4) → insurance fund → treasury queue, in that order.

T0 is hysteresis, not a partial liquidation: an account knocked into the warning zone by a brief spike gets one consensus block (~100 ms) to add margin or de-leverage before the engine escalates — the on-chain analogue of a broker's margin call, compressed to a block. The forced closes in T1 and T2 are floored at mark × (1 ∓ floor) — by default half the maintenance ratio — so a liquidation is never sold into a thin book; whatever cannot fill at the floor escalates to netting instead of cascading. A default 50 bps liquidation fee, charged only from remaining positive equity, capitalizes the insurance fund. The decision logic is a deterministic pure function over exact fixed-point thresholds.

5.3 Dynamic risk parameters live (devnet)

Maintenance ratios, leverage caps, and funding parameters are not frozen constants. Margin tiers are notional-banded — leverage steps down and maintenance steps up as position size grows — and the bands auto-tune from 30-day realized volatility, as does a funding-rate multiplier. Governance can override any parameter per market within protocol bounds, and can vote a live market into strict isolation without cancelling a single order.

5.4 Auto-deleveraging as online learning preview

When the ladder still leaves negative equity, the loss must be socialized onto profitable accounts. The industry-standard heuristic — rank winners by a return-on-equity score and haircut down the queue — has a documented pathology: under stress it over-liquidates profitable traders, and its ranking is unstable, so two accounts with the same PnL can meet very different fates.

MetaFlux reframes deleveraging as a regret-minimizing online-learning problem, following recent published work. A one-dimensional online gradient descent learns a severity parameter — what fraction of the round's deficit to haircut — with a dynamic regret bound of the form √((1 + 2·P_T)·Σ D_t²). Given that budget, a deterministic pro-rata rule allocates the haircut across profitable accounts in proportion to their PnL capacity, with no solver and no floating point. Pro-rata carries built-in min-max fairness: every winner is haircut by the same percentage of capacity.

On a replay of the published October 2025 stress event, this design over-liquidated by roughly $3.4M where the production heuristic it replaces overshot by roughly $45M — about 13× less, and within ~30% of the offline optimum — with zero monotonicity violations and near-perfect rank stability, against the baseline's 11.4% violations and 0.34 stability. These are benchmark figures from a devnet replay of the published analysis, not production outcomes. An ADL precompile quotes the pro-rata clearing price to contracts.

5.5 Staking & slashing live (devnet)

Validator misbehaviour carries economic cost, and the parameters are live on devnet rather than aspirational. Double-signing — provable from two conflicting signed messages at the same height and round — slashes 5% of stake and jails the validator; voting for an invalid fork slashes 5% and removes it permanently; sustained downtime costs 0.1% and a jail term. Unbonding takes a 7-day lock during which the stake remains fully slashable, so "withdraw, then attack" stays punishable. Validators post a minimum self-bond of 100,000 MTF and may charge at most 20% commission. Staking economics follow the fee flywheel (§8.3): validators earn the 20% validator share of the fee buyback, and stakers earn by delegating to validators, who pass that share down as the dividend — fees fund validators, validators fund stakers.

06

Axis 3 — Execution layer

The EVM is the lingua franca of on-chain strategy, but a serial, general-purpose EVM is a poor fit for a high-throughput derivatives chain. MetaFlux keeps full EVM compatibility and makes the execution layer faster and derivatives-aware.

6.1 Parallel EVM live (devnet)

Transactions execute optimistically in parallel under a Block-STM executor. Every node derives the same conflict partition from transaction content, runs non-conflicting strata concurrently, and catches any mis-estimated access set by read-set re-validation and re-run — the committed result is byte-identical to a serial execution of the same block, and the partition is advisory: correctness never depends on it. A contract call no longer queues behind every unrelated transaction in front of it. Operational and tested on devnet; block cadence and gas ceilings may still be tuned before launch.

6.2 Derivatives-aware precompiles live (devnet)

A Solidity contract that wants a portfolio-margin number or top-of-book depth today has two bad options: reimplement the logic in EVM bytecode over data it pushed on-chain — expensive, and a second implementation that drifts from the canonical one — or round-trip through an action and wait a block. A precompile collapses both into a single native call priced far below the equivalent bytecode. That gas asymmetry is the moat: it lets on-chain market makers and structured-product contracts consume L1-native clearing primitives directly.

BandPrimitives
Derivatives 0x0900–0x0904portfolio-margin evaluation, vault NAV, ADL pro-rata pricing, mark-to-market settle, RFQ book depth.
Market data 0x0906–0x0908best bid/offer, aggregated L2 depth, inventory risk.
Write patha CoreWriter contract with ~20 actions: orders, transfers, staking, vault ops, RFQ submit, FBA configure, encrypted order submit, cross-chain send.

Status is precise. The five derivatives precompiles and three market-data reads are operational on devnet as stateless pure functions: the caller supplies a deterministic snapshot in calldata, outputs are byte-identical for identical input, and the math inside is the same engine code that margins live accounts. Reads backed directly by live Core state are the next step. Governance can disable an individual MetaFlux precompile by stake-weighted vote; the Ethereum-standard precompiles can never be touched.

6.3 Native cross-chain primitives live (devnet)

Cross-chain movement is a native action, not a contract convention. A Solidity contract queues an outbound transfer through the CoreWriter's cross-chain send action, and MetaBridge releases on the destination chain after the two-thirds validator co-signature and the dispute window (§3.3). Inbound, deposits and credits land as deterministic system transactions — exactly-once, FIFO within each round. What remains before mainnet is the external audit and more corridors, not the mechanism.

08

Token & fee economics

MTF is the native token: it secures the chain through staking, it is the unit validators and their stakers earn, and protocol fees route value back to holders through a buyback flywheel. The design has three load-bearing ideas — a supply that tracks a living reference rather than an arbitrary cap, a fee engine that converts every basis point of revenue into buy pressure, and a staking ladder that pays the trader who locks the longest.

8.1 Supply — population-pegged

MTF does not have a fixed maximum supply, and it does not have an open-ended emissions schedule either. Total supply is pegged to the population of China: it launches at 1,404,890,000 MTF — one token for each person counted in China's 2026 population — and is re-pegged once a year so the float tracks the living population rather than a number chosen at genesis.

The peg is a governed, evidence-based process, not an oracle the team controls. Each year the reference figure is the median across multiple authoritative Chinese central-government data sources, which removes any single source's revision or rounding from the result. The validator set then votes to settle the difference: if the population grew, validators vote to mint the marginal MTF; if it shrank, they vote to burn an equal amount of treasury-held MTF. Mint and burn touch only the governed treasury pool — never user, contributor, or staker balances. Supply therefore breathes with the reference population — bounded, auditable, and never inflated by discretion. The annual re-peg is a small adjustment against a billion-token base, so circulating supply is effectively stable while remaining honestly anchored to something real.

8.2 Allocation

The full 1,404,890,000 MTF is allocated across three buckets, weighted toward the people who actually use and secure the network:

AllocationShareTerms

The contributor cliff and long vesting are deliberate: no insider can sell into the airdrop, and the team's tokens unlock over a horizon longer than any single market cycle.

8.3 Fee value-accrual — the flywheel

Every protocol fee is converted into buy pressure on MTF. The flow is mechanical, not discretionary. Fees are collected; maker rebates are paid first — along with referral and builder credits, settled off the top — so liquidity providers are made whole before anything else happens; then all remaining fee revenue buys MTF on the open market, under an anti-manipulation price cap, so in a fast market the buyback may lag rather than chase. That bought-back MTF — not the raw fee currency — is what gets distributed, split three ways:

70%Burned — permanently removed from supply. Continuous open-market buy pressure plus deflation, both returned to every holder.
20%Validators — routed to validators, who distribute it to their stakers as the revenue-share dividend.
10%Treasury — the governed pool that funds development, security, and ecosystem growth.

The flywheel turns trading volume directly into MTF demand and supply contraction, while paying the stakers who secure the chain out of the same revenue. Because the buyback executes on the open market, fee accrual is fully transparent on-chain and cannot be diluted by off-book issuance.

8.4 Fee structure

A trader's effective fee is set by three independent tier systems that stack — a desk can sit in a high volume tier, a high maker-rebate tier, and a high staking-discount tier at once, and each applies on its own axis:

SystemKeyed onRange
Fee tiers30-day total volumeTaker 0.035% → 0.020%; maker 0.010% → 0.000% as volume climbs.
Maker rebate tiersShare of total maker volumeUp to −0.003% for the venue's largest liquidity providers, paid before the buyback — net maker fees can go negative.
Staking discount tiersve-weighted MTF stakeA taker-only discount from 5% up to 50% — the staking ladder of §8.5.

Volume rewards flow; maker-share rewards depth; the staking ladder rewards conviction. Keeping the three orthogonal means a small but committed staker and a large but unstaked desk are each rewarded for the thing they actually bring.

Three optional fee redirections sit on top, each capped against abuse. A referrer earns a share of the taker fee carved from the protocol's cut rather than added to what the user pays; the referrer is single-level and set once, immutably, to remove an arbitrage surface. A builder code lets a third-party frontend or strategy provider charge a hard-capped per-fill fee with the trader's explicit on-chain approval. A MIP-3 deployer can set a fee on the markets it lists, under a governance-set cap — an incentive to deploy, bounded so a deployed market cannot become a fee trap. In the worst case where all three apply, total cost is bounded and disclosed pre-trade; the base case is just the protocol fee.

8.5 Staking — the administrative ladder

Staking MTF buys two things: a share of the validator dividend, and a taker fee discount that escalates with the size and conviction of the stake. The discount is delivered through a ten-rung ladder named for the Chinese administrative hierarchy — climbing from a township office to the top of the state — with each rung granting a larger cut of the taker fee.

Tier is determined by ve-weighted stake, not raw balance. A stake's effective weight is the staked amount multiplied by a lock-time multiplier, so locking longer counts for more: a flexible, no-lock stake carries a 0× multiplier — lowest tier, no dividend, the deliberate lane for market makers who need their capital mobile; a 1-month lock counts at 1.0×, a 6-month lock at 2.5×, and a 24-month lock at the 4.0× cap. Time-weighting is what makes the discount earned rather than bought: a long-locked holder outranks a larger holder who keeps the option to leave.

Every rung except one is uncapped — pure threshold, so any number of stakers who clear the ve-weight bar hold the title, including Minister. The single exception is the top: Premier / President / General Secretary is a competitive seat with a cap of one, held by the single largest holder by ve weight. It is the one rung you take from someone else.

The validator-routed 20% dividend requires at least a one-month lock — the flexible lane trades the dividend for mobility. Two network timing parameters govern how a stake moves: an exit cooldown on unstaking and a fee-discount activation delay before a new tier takes effect. Both are governance-set with a hard 24-hour code floor (the devnet unbonding default currently runs seven days), so no parameter change can make staking instantly reversible or a discount retroactive enough to game. The lock period itself is the staker's own choice: flexible, or one month and up to earn the dividend.

Security model

MetaFlux is a stake-weighted BFT L1. The relevant adversary thresholds are one-third and two-thirds of stake, not a Nakamoto "51%". There is no probabilistic finality and no reorg window.

Byzantine stakeSafetyLivenessConsequence
< 1/3holdsholdsNormal operation.
[1/3, 2/3)holdsattackableCensorship or halt — but no conflicting finality can be forged.
≥ 2/3broken—Conflicting blocks could finalize. This is the BFT limit.

No code can make a two-thirds colluding quorum safe; that is the boundary of any BFT protocol. Defense at and above the thresholds is economic and social — stake decentralization, slashing, transparency — not algorithmic. MetaFlux is candid about which threats matter most for an exchange specifically:

  • Censorship of liquidations (≥ 1/3) is the most reachable attack and the most under-appreciated: a one-third coalition can selectively censor time-critical actions, notably suppressing its own liquidations to push bad debt onto the protocol. The planned defense is forced-inclusion rules — a proposal is invalid if it omits eligible time-critical actions that were available. This lives in proposal-validity rules, above the commit rule, and is design intent today, not shipped code.
  • Consensus safety break (≥ 2/3) is the textbook double-spend path. It is deterred economically — double-signing is provable from two conflicting signatures and is slashed and jailed with self-verifying evidence — and structurally by the formal specification of the safety kernel. Slashing only deters a rational attacker; a token-short-then-attack adversary is not, which is why stake decentralization is the real defense.
  • Bridge / cross-chain double-spend is the largest concrete surface. Every MetaBridge transfer — USDC included — is validator-cosigned to two-thirds and passes a dispute window before release, and there is no admin key to steal. The bridge is live on its first corridors but pre-audit, and is explicitly marked not for value-bearing use until the external audit closes. That audit is the gating item before mainnet.
  • Early-launch centralization is an inherent risk: a small genesis validator set makes the supermajority threshold reachable. The mitigation is a credible progressive-decentralization roadmap and transparency, not code.

The consensus model is formally specified, with TLA+ / Stateright verification covering safety and liveness invariants and the production implementation audited against the spec. Determinism is enforced mechanically — floating point, unordered iteration, and wall-clock time are prohibited in the consensus path — and release binaries are GPG-signed against a pinned offline root key. If the chain halts, nothing is lost: reads keep serving the last finalized state, writes refuse cleanly, liquidations pause, and validators resume by replay. Vulnerability disclosure runs through security@mtf.exchange with a 48-hour acknowledgment and 90-day coordinated disclosure.

Roadmap

MetaFlux is pre-mainnet but not pre-code: devnet is public, stable on the wire, and open for integration today. The plan is milestone-gated, not calendar-driven, and progresses through testnet stability and audit closure before any mainnet.

Shipped — devnet

Consensus, clearinghouse, match engine, state hashing, staking, vaults, portfolio margin, tiered liquidation, the inline parallel EVM with derivatives precompiles, and the gateway — live and open for integration.

In flight

RFQ and FBA wire actions (the reads are live), ADL hardening from preview, spot margin and Earn calibration, the multi-sig execute path, Metaliquidity, threshold-encrypted intents, the Arbitrum bridge corridor.

Testnet & hardening

A public testnet with operator-run validators, sustained multi-week stress on the liquidation and bridge paths, and the six-month season that precedes the TGE airdrop.

Audit & launch

The external bridge audit — the explicit gating item — plus protocol audits against the formal spec, phased validator onboarding, and mainnet with the TGE.

MetaFlux is a bet that the next generation of derivatives infrastructure is won on capability, built deterministically, and proven honestly. Derivatives, on first principles.
